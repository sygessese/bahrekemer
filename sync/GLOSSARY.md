# Translation guide — Amharic → English

Read this before translating anything for the site. Look at the existing English in `content/` for the same page as the model of tone: it is the established voice, and new English must sit beside it without a seam.

## Voice

- Dignified, plain, slightly formal English of a learned elder — not academic jargon, not modern casual. Think of a careful translation of a scholar's essay, not a blog post.
- Faithful first: keep his images, comparisons and repetitions (they are deliberate). Do not summarise, soften criticism, or add explanations he didn't write.
- Keep his paragraph and heading structure exactly: one English block for each Amharic block.
- Poems: one English line per Amharic line, plain (no forced rhyme). Keep the order of images.
- Ge'ez quotations: give the Ge'ez transliterated in italics, then the meaning, as in the existing pages, e.g. *bizhu wetebazhu* (“Be fruitful and multiply”).
- Numbers written in Ge'ez numerals (፩ ፲ ፻ ፼ …) stay in Ge'ez numerals in the English too. Years: Ethiopian calendar, written "2016 E.C." (add the Gregorian in brackets only for a book's year line, e.g. "2016 E.C. (2023/24)").
- Curly quotation marks “ ” and apostrophe ’. Dashes are spaced em dashes: "word — word".
